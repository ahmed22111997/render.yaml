How to Use Podcast TTS Studio

Everything you need to write a script and get a finished episode.

1. Write your script

Each line starts with a speaker label followed by a colon, for example:

Host: Welcome back to the show.
Guest: Thanks for having me.

A line with no label continues the previous speaker's turn, so you can write a long paragraph across several lines without repeating the name. You can use up to four different speakers per episode.

2. Leave out anything you don't want spoken

Put stage directions or notes in square brackets, like [laughs] or [music fades in] — text inside brackets is skipped by the narrator. Markdown symbols such as *, #, and ` are also stripped automatically.

3. Pick a voice per speaker

Filter the voice list by language, then assign a distinct voice, speaking speed, and pitch to each speaker. Mixing a lower-pitched and a higher-pitched voice usually makes it easiest for listeners to tell speakers apart.

4. Add delivery and emotion (optional)

Put an emotion in parentheses right after a speaker's name to apply it to that whole turn:

Guest (excited): Thanks for having me! It's a strange story.

Or switch emotion partway through a turn with a curly-brace tag, and insert a timed pause the same way:

Guest: Quite a lot. {pause 1.0} {sad} Your brain replays the day.

Available emotions include neutral, happy, excited, curious, surprised, serious, thoughtful, calm, and sad (plus a few common synonyms like "cheerful" or "gentle"). These presets adjust speaking speed, pitch, and volume — they approximate a mood rather than a full voice-acting performance, so it's worth generating a short test line first and adjusting the "emotion strength" slider to taste.

5. Generate and download

Press "Generate episode." Longer scripts take longer because each chunk of speech is synthesized in sequence. When it finishes, you can preview the result in the built-in player and download the MP3, WAV, and — if you checked the subtitles option — an SRT file with per-line timestamps.

FAQ

Is this free?

Yes. There's no account, no sign-up, and no usage cap beyond keeping scripts to a reasonable length.

Are the voices real people?

No — they're general-purpose synthetic neural voices, not recordings or clones of specific individuals.

How long are my files kept?

Generated files are temporary and are automatically removed a few hours after creation, so download what you need right away.

Can I use the audio commercially?

You're responsible for how you use audio you generate; see the Terms of Service for acceptable use.
